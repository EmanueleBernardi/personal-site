About me

I recently obtained my PhD in Pure and Applied Mathematics at Politecnico di Torino.

I am broadly interested in the mathematical description of complex systems composed of a large number of interacting individuals, and in how collective behavior emerges from individual-level dynamics.

My research focuses on the mathematical modeling of heterogeneous, evolving populations, using phenotype-structured integro-differential models that combine analytical methods with stochastic individual-based simulations. This multi-scale approach bridges microscopic, mesoscopic, and macroscopic descriptions of biological systems, with applications in epidemiology and population dynamics, and includes calibrating models against real data.

On the computational side, I implemented MATLAB solvers for integro-differential, partial differential, and ordinary differential equations, as well as Monte Carlo methods for stochastic simulations. I also have working knowledge of Python, R, and Git.

Outside mathematics, I enjoy comics, video games, and animation.
